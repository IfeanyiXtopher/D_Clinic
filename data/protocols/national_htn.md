# National hypertension protocol (PHC educational adaptation)

Original educational text for this MVP, shaped like a primary-health-care
hypertension program in Nigeria that follows a HEARTS-style ladder. It is not
an official Federal Ministry of Health gazette and must not be cited as law.

## N-01 Who this protocol is for

Adults 18 years and older with hypertension enrolled at a primary health
centre or general outpatient clinic. Children and pregnant women are
referred. Identity (name, phone, address, national ID) is never written into
a protocol question.

## N-02 Confirming hypertension at PHC

Two clinic readings on different days at or above 140/90, or one reading at
or above 160/100 plus a clinical decision, may start treatment under the
clinician. A single SMS report of a home reading is not enough to step up.

## N-03 First-line at PHC

The first-line protocol drug in this adaptation is amlodipine 5 mg daily,
matching HEARTS step 1. Calcium-channel blockers are widely available in the
program formulary. Do not substitute losartan or lisinopril from this tool.

## N-04 Two-drug combination at PHC

When amlodipine 10 mg is not enough, add telmisartan 40 mg (then 80 mg) as in
HEARTS steps 3 and 4. Keep both once daily if the patient can swallow the
tablets. Record the combination on the prescription card the clinician signs.

## N-05 Thiazide-like diuretic

Chlorthalidone 12.5 mg is the third protocol agent (HEARTS step 5). Prefer
morning dosing. If chlorthalidone is out of stock, the clinician may use
another thiazide from the local formulary — this tool will not name a
substitute.

## N-06 Nurse and community-health role

Nurses and CHEWs measure blood pressure, support adherence, run the overdue
list, and give lifestyle advice. They do not independently create a new
prescription from the ready-reckoner. A medical officer or authorized
prescriber signs any step-up.

## N-07 Refill and 28-day supply

Where stock allows, give at least a 28-day supply of protocol drugs at a
controlled visit. Short supplies increase missed doses. An overdue patient
needs a return visit, not a remote extra bottle ordered by the model.

## N-08 Side-effect watch at PHC

Watch for ankle swelling on amlodipine, dizziness after a step-up, and
dehydration on chlorthalidone in hot weather. Face swelling or severe
breathlessness is emergency referral. Do not stop all drugs from an SMS.

## N-09 Diabetes at the same PHC

If the patient also has diabetes, keep the hypertension ladder and the 140/90
target. Metformin 500 mg, when already prescribed, is unrelated to the next
hypertension step. Do not start insulin from this protocol.

## N-10 Documentation

Record systolic, diastolic, current protocol drugs, and the next appointment.
The ready-reckoner citation (chunk id such as N-03 or H-05) can go in the
staff note. Never paste a patient name into the question box.
