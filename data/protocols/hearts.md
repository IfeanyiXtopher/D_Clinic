# HEARTS hypertension protocol (educational adaptation)

This text is an original educational adaptation of the publicly described WHO
HEARTS treatment approach for adult hypertension at primary care. It is not
the WHO manual, not a national legal protocol, and not a prescription. A
clinician reviews every step. Identity never belongs in a question to this
tool.

## H-01 Blood pressure target

The program target for most adults on treatment is a clinic blood pressure
below 140/90 mmHg. A latest reading of 139/89 or lower is treated as
controlled. A reading of 140/90 or higher is above target and should be
confirmed before any drug change. This tool does not diagnose.

## H-02 How to measure blood pressure

Measure blood pressure with the patient seated, arm supported, after at least
five minutes of rest. Use a validated cuff that fits the upper arm. Record
systolic and diastolic. If the first reading is 140/90 or higher, repeat after
a few minutes and use the lower of the two for the visit decision. Do not
change drugs from a single home reading reported by SMS.

## H-03 Lifestyle advice at every visit

Advise reduced salt, more vegetables, less fried food, walking most days,
stopping tobacco, and limiting alcohol. Lifestyle sits beside drugs; it does
not replace a needed step-up when blood pressure stays at 140/90 or higher
after confirmation.

## H-04 Step 1 amlodipine 5 mg

If hypertension is confirmed and the patient is not yet on a protocol drug,
the first HEARTS-style step used in this program is amlodipine 5 mg once
daily. This is a protocol suggestion for the clinician, not a prescription
from the model.

## H-05 Step 2 amlodipine 10 mg

If the patient is already on amlodipine 5 mg and confirmed clinic blood
pressure remains 140/90 or higher, the next protocol step is amlodipine 10 mg
once daily. Check ankle swelling. Do not skip a confirmatory reading.

## H-06 Step 3 add telmisartan 40 mg

If the patient is on amlodipine 10 mg and confirmed blood pressure remains
140/90 or higher, the next protocol step is to add telmisartan 40 mg once
daily (two-drug combination). Do not start telmisartan in pregnancy — refer.

## H-07 Step 4 telmisartan 80 mg

If the patient is on amlodipine 10 mg plus telmisartan 40 mg and confirmed
blood pressure remains 140/90 or higher, the next protocol step is
telmisartan 80 mg once daily with amlodipine 10 mg.

## H-08 Step 5 add chlorthalidone 12.5 mg

If the patient is on amlodipine 10 mg plus telmisartan 80 mg and confirmed
blood pressure remains 140/90 or higher, the next protocol step is to add
chlorthalidone 12.5 mg once daily. Check electrolytes when the facility can.
If still uncontrolled on three protocol drugs, refer.

## H-09 Controlled blood pressure

If the latest confirmed clinic blood pressure is below 140/90, continue the
current protocol drugs. Do not step up. Review adherence and the next visit
date. This summary must not invent a new drug.

## H-10 Very high blood pressure

If clinic systolic is 180 or higher, or diastolic is 120 or higher, this is
urgent. Recheck. Look for symptoms such as chest pain, severe headache, or
confusion. Refer the same day. Do not titrate drugs by SMS or from this
ready-reckoner.

## H-11 Follow-up interval

After a drug start or step-up, review in about four weeks with a clinic blood
pressure. If controlled and stable, many programs review every two to three
months. Overdue patients need a call, not a remote dose change.

## H-12 Missed tablets

If a patient missed a dose, they should take the next dose at the usual time.
They should not take two doses at once. If many days were missed, the
clinician reviews at the visit. This tool does not restart or stop treatment.

## H-13 Amlodipine ankle swelling

Ankle swelling can occur on amlodipine. It is not a reason for this tool to
stop the drug. The clinician may review the dose or the combination at the
next visit. Urgent swelling of the face or difficulty breathing is an
emergency — refer.

## H-14 Diabetes and hypertension

Adults with both diabetes and hypertension still use the 140/90 clinic target
in this program unless a clinician has documented a different goal. The same
amlodipine–telmisartan–chlorthalidone ladder applies. Metformin is a diabetes
drug and is not a HEARTS hypertension step.

## H-15 Adherence before a step-up

Before stepping up, ask whether tablets were taken most days. Poor adherence
is a reason to support the patient, not to add a third drug from this tool.
Confirm the reading and the refill.

## H-16 When this tool must refuse

This ready-reckoner answers only adult hypertension and related follow-up
from the protocol chunks. It does not treat pregnancy, children, cancer,
infections, injuries, or mental health. For those, say the topic is not
covered and the clinician should use the right guideline.

## H-17 Pregnancy

Pregnant patients with high blood pressure must be referred. This protocol
ladder (amlodipine, telmisartan, chlorthalidone) is not for pregnancy.
Telmisartan must not be used in pregnancy. The ready-reckoner will not choose
a pregnancy drug.

## H-18 Referral

Refer if blood pressure stays 140/90 or higher on three protocol drugs, if
there is suspected heart failure, stroke, pregnancy, age under 18, or
hypertensive urgency (180/120 or symptoms). The worker records the referral;
the model does not name a hospital.

## H-19 What the worker may tell the patient

The worker may explain the 140/90 target, the current tablet names already on
the record, and the next visit. The worker must not read a new dose from this
tool as if it were already prescribed. Every step-up is a clinician decision.

## H-20 Blood sugar is not a hypertension step

A high blood sugar or HbA1c does not by itself change the HEARTS
hypertension ladder. Continue protocol blood-pressure drugs and use the
diabetes pathway separately. Do not invent insulin or a new diabetes dose
here.
